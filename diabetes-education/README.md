## v2: Personal plan edition (`my-plan.html`)

- **Where answers go:** they stay in the learner's browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere. Every question is optional. The two versions save progress separately.
- **Medical details:** the learner reports them, and the plan marks them "confirm with your care team." The plan never suggests changing a medicine.
- **What it asks about:** age, other health conditions, last A1C, how they check blood sugar, personal goals, eating pattern (vegan through meat-eater), home cuisines, who cooks, food insecurity (one Hunger Vital Sign item), sugary drinks, schedule and shift work, current activity, physical limits, activities they enjoy, a safe place to walk, diabetes medicines, trouble taking medicines, fast sugar and glucagon (asked only if they take insulin or a sulfonylurea), living alone, driving, phone numbers, tobacco, transportation, costs, how overwhelmed they feel, PHQ-2, faith practices (Sabbath, fasting, prayer, faith community), and the support they want.
- **The plan has these sections:**
  - About me
  - My numbers
  - My eating plan, with meal ideas matched to their eating pattern and home cuisines
  - My activity plan, adjusted for physical limits
  - My medicines, with tips for each medicine they take
  - My safety plan: the low blood sugar steps appear in full only if they take insulin or a sulfonylurea, and the sick-day steps match their medicines
  - My checkups
  - Coping and support
  - Faith and rest, shown only if they opt in, plus a fasting safety box whenever they pick fasting
  - My first step this week
  - Questions for my care team, written from their answers
  - A care team sign-off box
- **Family and support (Part 1):** who lives with them, their support circle, main helpers, whether family knows about the diabetes, how they want help (reminders, cooking, being active, appointments, emergencies, encouragement, rides and costs, or "on my own for now"), whether they care for others, family history, and an emergency contact. This builds a **My support team** section that turns each kind of help into a concrete suggestion.
- **My daily log** (📝 in the header and side menu): for each day, the learner logs
  - food (what they ate, plus taps for half-plate veggies, whole grains, plant protein, water)
  - activity minutes
  - medicines (from their medicine answers, plus any they add, like blood pressure pills)
  - blood sugar readings, each with a friendly label
  - mood and notes

  A **My week** summary shows badges and progress toward their personal activity goal. The weekly summary also appears in the plan, and any logged lows are added to the care team questions. Learners can print their week, or print a blank paper log if they don't use a phone.
- **Tone:** encouraging throughout. Missed doses, high numbers and hard days get supportive wording and practical next steps, never blame. Safety rules (nothing by mouth if someone has passed out, never share insulin pens) stay clearly stated.
- **Print:** "Print or save my plan (PDF)" prints only the plan.

A 60-minute, self-paced, interactive learning page for adults with type 2 diabetes and their families.

- **Audience:** patients with type 2 diabetes and their family members or helpers
- **Reading level:** written for grades 6–8 (short sentences, everyday words)
- **Approach:** plant-forward eating (not plant-only); optional faith-informed reflection moments that learners can hide
- **Framework:** ADCES7 Self-Care Behaviors, aligned with the 2022 National Standards for DSMES
- **Format:** one self-contained file (`index.html`). Open it in any web browser. No server or account needed. Progress is saved only in the learner's own browser.

## Structure (60 minutes)

| # | Part | Min | ADCES7 behavior | Interactive pieces |
|---|------|-----|-----------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Welcome | 4 | Goal setting | Who is learning, personal "why," 5-question start check, confidence rating |
| 2 | What is type 2 diabetes? | 6 | (Foundations) | Insulin "key" diagram, myth/fact, quiz |
| 3 | Knowing your numbers | 7 | Monitoring | Blood sugar number checker, CGM graph reading, quiz |
| 4 | Healthy, plant-forward eating | 9 | Healthy eating | Plate builder, food label activity, quiz |
| 5 | Moving more | 5 | Being active | Weekly activity planner, quiz |
| 6 | Taking your medicines | 7 | Taking medication | Medicine matching, quiz |
| 7 | Lows, highs, and sick days | 9 | Problem solving | Rule of 15 walk-through, 3 family scenarios, quiz |
| 8 | Protecting your body | 5 | Reducing risks | Yearly care checklist, foot care, quiz |
| 9 | Coping and support | 3 | Healthy coping | Family "helpful / not so helpful" sort |
| 10 | Your plan | 5 | All | Teach-back, final check (scored against start check), SMART action plan, printable plan + low and sick-day cards |

Each part includes learning goals, a "How family can help" box, and (where it fits) an optional reflection moment.

## Clinical review checklist (before patient use)

A CDCES and a physician reviewer should confirm or adjust these items to match LLUH protocols:

- [ ] Glucose targets (80–130 before meals, <180 after, A1C <7%, TIR >70%, TBR <4%)
- [ ] **Sick-day call thresholds** ("over 300 twice in a row, or over 250 for more than a day"; vomiting/diarrhea >6 h; fever >101°F for more than a day)
- [ ] Sick-day guidance on pausing metformin and SGLT2 inhibitors, and checking ketones
- [ ] Sick-day carb guidance (~15 g/hour) and fluids (~1 cup/hour)
- [ ] Medicine table (classes, examples, key points) against the current formulary
- [ ] Insulin storage wording (~28 days at room temperature once opened, check the label)
- [ ] Vaccine list against current CDC adult schedule
- [ ] Screening intervals (eye, kidney, foot, dental, lipids)
- [ ] Reflection moments and scripture (3 John 1:2) reviewed by Mission/Spiritual Care
- [ ] Readability check (e.g., Flesch-Kincaid) and PEMAT understandability/actionability scoring
- [ ] Usability test with 3–5 patients and family members
- [ ] Add LLUH contact numbers, DSMES referral info, and approved branding (not included in this draft)
